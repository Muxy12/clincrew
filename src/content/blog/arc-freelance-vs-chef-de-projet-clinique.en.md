Choosing between a freelance CRA and a clinical project manager comes down to one question: do you need to secure the field, or to run the whole study? Both profiles are complementary, but their scope clearly differs.

## What a freelance CRA does

A Clinical Research Associate (CRA) works close to the investigational sites. They are responsible for site oversight: [monitoring visits](/en/services/visites-de-monitoring), data verification, protocol and GCP compliance, documentation and deviation management. Their scope is monitoring and quality at site level.

## What a clinical project manager does

A clinical project manager (CPM) leads the study as a whole: timelines, budget, risk management, vendor and team coordination (CRAs, data management, pharmacovigilance, regulatory) and the interface with the sponsor. They own delivery against time, cost and quality at project level.

## The main differences

- **Scale**: the CRA acts at site level; the CPM at project level.
- **Responsibility**: data quality and compliance for the CRA; timelines, budget and coordination for the CPM.
- **Field**: the CRA runs the visits; the CPM steers and arbitrates.

## Which one to choose?

If your priority is securing site monitoring and data quality, a freelance CRA is the right choice. If you need to lead the entire study and coordinate several functions, a clinical project manager is more suitable. Some profiles, including mine, can work across both and advise on the most relevant setup.

## In short

- Freelance CRA: monitoring, quality and documentation at site level.
- Project manager: end-to-end leadership (time, budget, risk, coordination).
- The right choice depends on your need: field or steering.
