A CPP (Comité de Protection des Personnes) is an independent ethics committee that reviews research projects involving human participants before they start. In France, no clinical trial can begin without its favourable opinion. Here, in more detail, is its role and how it works.

## Its mission

The ethics committee protects participants. It verifies that the research is ethically acceptable and properly designed before a single person is enrolled. For a short definition, see the glossary fiche on the [ethics committee](/en/glossaire/cpp).

## What it reviews

- The protection of participants and the quality of the information given to them
- [Informed consent](/en/glossaire/consentement-eclaire) and its suitability for the research
- Scientific relevance and methodology
- The protection of personal data

## Its composition

An ethics committee brings together healthcare professionals (doctors, pharmacists, nurses, etc.) and members of civil society (lawyers, patient representatives, people qualified in ethics). This diversity ensures both a scientific and a citizen perspective.

## How it works

Since the [Jardé law](/en/blog/loi-jarde), the committee is designated by random draw, for independence. Its favourable opinion is essential and, for medicines, is added to the [ANSM](/en/glossaire/ansm) authorisation.

## Ethics committee and the trial

Once the opinion is given, any significant amendment to the [protocol](/en/glossaire/protocole) may require a new review. Compliance with this framework is verified throughout the study, notably during [monitoring visits](/en/services/visites-de-monitoring).

*Reference: Jardé law; French public health code.*
