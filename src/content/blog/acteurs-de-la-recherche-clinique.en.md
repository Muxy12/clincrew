A clinical trial brings together many players, each with a precise role. Understanding who does what means understanding how a study is designed, authorised, conducted and overseen. Here are the main players and how they fit together.

## The sponsor: initiating the study

The [sponsor](/en/glossaire/promoteur) (pharmaceutical company, biotech or academic sponsor) initiates the trial and takes responsibility for it: protocol, funding, authorisations and decisions.

## The CRO: the operational provider

The sponsor often entrusts all or part of the conduct to a [CRO](/en/glossaire/cro), which supplies teams (monitoring, data management, regulatory). A CRO can reinforce its teams with a [freelance CRA](/en/solutions/cra-freelance-cro).

## The CRA: the link at the sites

The [Clinical Research Associate](/en/blog/qu-est-ce-qu-un-arc-cra) handles monitoring. A distinction is made between the [sponsor CRA](/en/glossaire/arc-promoteur), who monitors from outside on behalf of the sponsor, and the [site CRA](/en/glossaire/arc-investigateur), who works within the site.

## The investigator and the technician: at the heart of the site

The [investigator](/en/glossaire/investigateur) is the physician responsible for the study at the site. They are often supported by a [clinical trial technician](/en/glossaire/tec), who handles logistics and data entry.

## The project manager and the data manager

The [clinical project manager](/en/glossaire/chef-de-projet-clinique) steers the study as a whole; the [data manager](/en/glossaire/data-manager) designs and controls the database.

## The authorities and committees

A trial does not exist without a framework: the [ANSM](/en/glossaire/ansm) authorises, the [ethics committee](/en/glossaire/cpp) gives an opinion, the [EMA](/en/glossaire/ema) coordinates at European level.

## In short

From the sponsor to the authorities, each player contributes to participant safety and data reliability. The CRA is the daily link in the field.
