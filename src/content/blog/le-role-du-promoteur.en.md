The sponsor is the individual or organisation that initiates a clinical trial and takes responsibility for it. It is the cornerstone of the study: without a sponsor, there is no trial. For a short definition, see the glossary fiche on the [sponsor](/en/glossaire/promoteur); this article details its role.

## Who can be a sponsor

A sponsor can be a pharmaceutical company, a biotech, but also a healthcare facility or a university (then called academic or institutional sponsorship). In all cases, it carries the regulatory responsibility for the trial.

## Its responsibilities

- Design the [protocol](/en/glossaire/protocole) and obtain the authorisations ([ANSM](/en/glossaire/ansm), [ethics committee](/en/glossaire/cpp) opinion)
- Select the sites and the vendors
- Safeguard participant safety and data integrity
- Ensure pharmacovigilance and the reporting of [SUSARs](/en/blog/susar-recherche-clinique)
- Keep and archive the trial documentation

## Delegating monitoring

The sponsor keeps the responsibility, but often delegates the operational conduct to a [CRO](/en/glossaire/cro) or to Clinical Research Associates. It can also call on a [freelance CRA](/en/solutions/monitoring-promoteur) to support its internal team.

## Sponsor and CRA

The CRA always acts on behalf of the sponsor (or the CRO representing it). It is the CRA who, in the field, applies the decisions and procedures defined by the sponsor.
