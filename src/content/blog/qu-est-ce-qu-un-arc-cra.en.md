A Clinical Research Associate (CRA), also called a clinical monitor, is the professional who oversees the conduct of a clinical trial on behalf of the sponsor. The CRA makes sure participants' rights and safety are protected, that the data collected are reliable, and that the study complies with the protocol, Good Clinical Practice (ICH-GCP) and applicable regulations.

## The role of the CRA in a clinical trial

The CRA is the main link between the sponsor and the investigational sites. They qualify and initiate sites, run monitoring visits, verify data against source documents, follow safety reporting, check investigational product management and keep the trial documentation up to date.

## Main duties

- Site selection and initiation.
- [Monitoring visits](/en/services/visites-de-monitoring): consent, eligibility and source data verification.
- Safety and serious adverse event follow-up.
- Data quality control and query management.
- [Documentation upkeep](/en/services/controle-documentation) (TMF / eTMF, investigator site file).

## Skills required

The role calls for a scientific or healthcare background, solid knowledge of GCP and regulations, rigor, strong organisation, good communication and writing skills, and fluency in English.

## Sponsor CRA or site CRA?

The sponsor CRA (or CRO CRA) monitors sites on behalf of the study sponsor. The site-based associate, often a study coordinator or CTA, works within the healthcare facility alongside the investigator, helping collect data and organise enrolment. The two roles are complementary but distinct.

## CRA or ARC?

ARC (Attaché de Recherche Clinique) is the French term; CRA (Clinical Research Associate) its English equivalent. Both describe the same job. CRA is more common on international studies.

## In short

- The CRA monitors the trial for the sponsor and protects data quality.
- The scope covers selection, monitoring, quality and documentation.
- Sponsor CRA and site CRA are two different roles.
