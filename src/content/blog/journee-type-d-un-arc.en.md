What does a monitoring day actually look like? While every study and every site is different, a visit day often follows the same thread. Here is the typical flow of a Clinical Research Associate's day in the field.

## Before the visit: preparation

It all starts beforehand. You review the data already reported, the open items from the last visit and the [monitoring plan](/en/glossaire/plan-de-monitoring) to focus on what matters. A well-prepared visit is an efficient visit.

## At the site: checking what matters

On site, the CRA verifies participant safety and data quality: presence and dating of consent forms, agreement between source data and the case report form ([SDV](/en/glossaire/sdv)), investigational product management, adverse event reporting. Critical data is prioritised over a line-by-line check.

## With the team: exchanging and supporting

A visit is not only a check. It is also time to engage with the investigator and the site team: answering questions, clearing blockers, recalling protocol points. The relationship of trust makes the difference over time.

## After the visit: reporting

Back at the desk, the CRA writes the [monitoring visit report](/en/glossaire/rapport-de-monitoring): findings, deviations, corrective actions and deadlines. It is the written record of the visit and the starting point for follow-up.

## Between visits: tracking actions

The work does not stop at the visit. Between visits, you track open items, send reminders, and keep the thread remotely. It is this continuous follow-up, more than the visit itself, that secures a study.
