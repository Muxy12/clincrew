The Jardé law governs, in France, research involving the human person (RIPH). In force since 2016, it unified and clarified the framework for research in humans, classifying studies by the level of risk to participants.

## The three research categories

The Jardé law distinguishes:

1. **Interventional research** (category 1), involving an intervention not justified by routine care. These are [clinical trials](/en/glossaire/essai-clinique) in the strict sense.
2. **Interventional research with minimal risks and constraints** (category 2).
3. **Non-interventional research** (category 3), or [observational](/en/blog/recherche-non-interventionnelle).

The level of oversight increases with the risk.

## The ethics committee's role

Whatever the category, any such research must receive the favourable opinion of a randomly assigned [ethics committee (CPP)](/en/glossaire/cpp). For medicine trials, the [ANSM](/en/glossaire/ansm) authorisation is added.

## What it changes in the field

The Jardé law clarified who does what and reinforced participant protection: information, [consent](/en/glossaire/consentement-eclaire) adapted to the category, and traceability. For the CRA, it is the French foundation that connects with [Good Clinical Practice](/en/glossaire/bonnes-pratiques-cliniques) and the [EU regulation](/en/glossaire/reglement-europeen-essais-cliniques).

For the full framework, see [clinical trial regulation](/en/blog/reglementation-des-essais-cliniques).

*Reference: law No 2012-300 (Jardé law); French public health code.*
