Working with a freelance CRA is simple, provided the assignment is scoped from the start. A clear scope prevents misunderstandings and lets the consultant be operational quickly. Here is what to clarify before kicking off.

## 1. Scope and deliverables

Define precisely what is entrusted: which sites, which visit types (selection, initiation, routine, close-out), what volume, what duration. Specify the **expected deliverables** (visit reports, action follow-up, reporting) and their timelines. The clearer the scope, the more effective the engagement.

## 2. Responsibilities and oversight

The CRA acts on behalf of the sponsor or CRO, in line with the protocol and Good Clinical Practice. **GCP (ICH E6)** provides that the sponsor remains responsible for the study and may delegate trial-related tasks, provided they are defined and overseen. So clarify who approves what, and under which procedures.

## 3. Access and tools

An assignment starts fast when access is ready: systems (eTMF, CTMS, EDC), accounts, VPN, permissions, and the study documentation. Anticipate these: they are often the first source of delay.

## 4. Practical arrangements

- **Schedule**: start dates, visit frequency, availability.
- **Travel**: on-site, hybrid or remote depending on the sites.
- **Communication**: points of contact, regular check-ins, channels.
- **Contract**: nature of the engagement, confidentiality, invoicing.

## In short

Good scoping comes down to four points: **scope and deliverables**, **responsibilities and oversight**, **access and tools**, **practical arrangements**. That is what lets a freelance CRA be autonomous from day one, without heavy supervision.
