Clinical research is not reserved for physicians. It relies on a team of varied profiles, scientific and operational, that complement each other. Here are the main jobs and the pathways that lead to them.

## The field jobs

- The [investigator](/en/glossaire/investigateur): the physician responsible for the study at a site.
- The [clinical research nurse](/en/blog/devenir-infirmiere-de-recherche-clinique) and the [technician](/en/glossaire/tec): they run the study at the site.
- The [Clinical Research Associate (CRA)](/en/blog/qu-est-ce-qu-un-arc-cra): handles site monitoring.

## The steering and data jobs

- The [clinical project manager](/en/glossaire/chef-de-projet-clinique): coordinates the study as a whole.
- The [data manager](/en/glossaire/data-manager) and biostatistician: ensure data quality and analysis.
- Regulatory and pharmacovigilance profiles.

## What pathways?

Entry routes are many: scientific training (biology, health, pharmacy), nursing schools, master's degrees and university diplomas in clinical research. Site experience and command of [Good Clinical Practice](/en/glossaire/bonnes-pratiques-cliniques) count as much as the initial degree.

## An open field

Many professionals reach clinical research through transition: a caregiver becomes a technician, a technician becomes a CRA, a CRA moves into project management. To understand the whole picture, see [the players in clinical research](/en/blog/acteurs-de-la-recherche-clinique).
