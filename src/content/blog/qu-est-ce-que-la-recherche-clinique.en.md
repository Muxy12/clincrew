Clinical research refers to all studies carried out in humans to better understand a disease, evaluate a treatment or improve care. It bridges scientific discovery and care: it is what proves, in humans, that a medicine is effective and safe.

## Its objective

Clinical research aims to produce reliable evidence. To do so, it relies on a rigorous methodology (a [protocol](/en/glossaire/protocole), [inclusion criteria](/en/glossaire/criteres-inclusion-exclusion), sometimes [randomisation](/en/glossaire/randomisation)) and on a strict ethical and regulatory framework, governed by [Good Clinical Practice](/en/glossaire/bonnes-pratiques-cliniques).

## Its main categories

A key distinction is made between:

- **Interventional research**, where a treatment is given or a procedure applied as part of the study (a [clinical trial](/en/glossaire/essai-clinique)).
- **Non-interventional research** (or observational), where care is observed without being modified.

In France, the [Jardé law](/en/blog/loi-jarde) classifies this research by the level of risk to participants.

## How a study unfolds

A trial is built in [phases](/en/glossaire/phases-essai-clinique), from first administration in humans to post-marketing follow-up. Its conduct in the field relies on [clinical monitoring](/en/glossaire/monitoring-clinique), carried out by a Clinical Research Associate at investigator sites.

## Who takes part

Many [players](/en/blog/acteurs-de-la-recherche-clinique) are involved: sponsor, CRO, investigator, CRA, data manager, authorities. Each contributes to participant safety and data reliability.
