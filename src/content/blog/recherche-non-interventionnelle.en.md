Non-interventional research, also called observational research, is a study in which participants are observed without their care being modified. Treatments are prescribed as in routine care: the research only observes and collects data.

## The principle

Unlike an [interventional study](/en/blog/etude-interventionnelle), no treatment is allocated as part of the study and no constraint is added. Information is collected about what actually happens, in real life.

## A few examples

- Cohort studies that follow a group of patients over time
- Disease or treatment registries
- Studies on already-existing data (medical records)

Such research provides data complementary to trials, notably in [phase IV](/en/glossaire/phases-essai-clinique), after marketing.

## A lighter but real framework

The level of constraint is lower, but a framework exists: personal data protection ([CNIL, GDPR](/en/blog/cnil-recherche-clinique)), participant information, and classification under the [Jardé law](/en/blog/loi-jarde). [Monitoring](/en/glossaire/monitoring-clinique) is often lighter than for an interventional trial.

## In short

Interventional, you act; non-interventional, you observe. The two approaches are complementary to understand a treatment's efficacy and safety.
