The clinical research nurse holds a key place within an investigator site. At the crossroads of care and research, they contribute to the smooth running of trials while safeguarding participants' well-being.

## Their day-to-day role

- Welcoming and following up participants at the visits planned in the [protocol](/en/glossaire/protocole)
- Performing the planned acts (sampling, administration, measurements)
- Collecting and entering data, often in the [eCRF](/en/blog/crf-ecrf-cahier-d-observation)
- Safeguarding participants' safety and rights

They work closely with the [investigator](/en/glossaire/investigateur) and the [technician](/en/glossaire/tec), and collaborate with the [CRA](/en/glossaire/arc-promoteur) during monitoring visits.

## The skills expected

Rigour, a sense of organisation, ease with IT tools, good rapport with patients, and knowledge of [Good Clinical Practice](/en/glossaire/bonnes-pratiques-cliniques).

## What training?

You first become a state-registered nurse. Specialising in clinical research then comes through site experience, GCP training and, sometimes, dedicated university diplomas in clinical research.

## Pathways to other jobs

Research-nurse experience opens pathways: towards the [technician](/en/glossaire/tec) role, then sometimes towards that of [Clinical Research Associate](/en/blog/qu-est-ce-qu-un-arc-cra). To understand all the profiles, see [who can work in clinical research](/en/blog/qui-peut-faire-de-la-recherche-clinique).

*Reference: investigator site organisation; ICH E6.*
