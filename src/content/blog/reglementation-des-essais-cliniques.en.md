A clinical trial does not exist without a regulatory framework. From design to close-out, it must comply with rules that protect participants and ensure the reliability of the data. Here are the main pillars of this regulation, in France and Europe, and the CRA's place in applying them.

## The foundation: Good Clinical Practice

Everything rests on [Good Clinical Practice (GCP / ICH-GCP)](/en/glossaire/bonnes-pratiques-cliniques), the international standard (ICH E6) that governs the ethical and scientific conduct of trials. It is the framework the CRA applies day to day.

## The authorities: ANSM and EMA

In France, [ANSM](/en/glossaire/ansm) authorises and oversees medicine trials. At European level, the [EMA](/en/glossaire/ema) coordinates assessment and supervision, through to the [marketing authorisation](/en/glossaire/amm).

## Ethics: the ethics committee

No trial starts without the favourable opinion of an [ethics committee](/en/glossaire/cpp), which safeguards the protection of participants and informed consent.

## The European framework: the CTR

The [EU Clinical Trials Regulation (CTR 536/2014)](/en/glossaire/reglement-europeen-essais-cliniques) harmonises the authorisation and conduct of multi-country trials through the CTIS portal.

## Safety: pharmacovigilance and vigilance

During and after the trial, [pharmacovigilance](/en/glossaire/pharmacovigilance) monitors [adverse events](/en/glossaire/evenement-indesirable). Compliance can be checked through an [audit or inspection](/en/glossaire/audit-et-inspection).

## The CRA's role

The CRA is the field guarantor of this framework: they verify compliance with the protocol and GCP during [monitoring visits](/en/services/visites-de-monitoring) and document any deviation.
