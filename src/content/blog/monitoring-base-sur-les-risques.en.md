Risk-based monitoring (RBM) focuses the oversight of a clinical trial on what truly matters: the data and processes critical to patient safety and result reliability. Introduced by ICH E6(R2) and reinforced by ICH E6(R3), it replaces the idea of checking every data point uniformly.

## Why monitoring evolved

Verifying 100% of the data, at every site, on every visit, takes a lot of time for sometimes limited gain. RBM starts from a simple fact: not all data carry the same risk. It is better to focus effort where an error would have the greatest impact.

## Key principles

- **Identify risks** upfront: critical data, critical processes, sensitive sites.
- **Adjust monitoring intensity** to each site's risk level.
- **Combine** on-site and [remote monitoring](/en/blog/monitoring-a-distance).
- **Escalate early** the weak signals to the study team.

## What it changes in practice

[Monitoring visits](/en/services/visites-de-monitoring) remain essential, but their content is targeted: critical source data, consent and safety come first, rather than a systematic line-by-line review. Key risk indicators (KRIs) guide the decisions.

## In short

- RBM refocuses monitoring on critical data and processes.
- It builds on ICH E6(R2) and E6(R3).
- It combines on-site and remote oversight, driven by risk.
