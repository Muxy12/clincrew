A SUSAR (Suspected Unexpected Serious Adverse Reaction) is a suspected serious and unexpected adverse reaction occurring in a clinical trial. It is one of the most important safety signals: its rapid identification and reporting protect participants, on the trial concerned and on others.

## Breaking down the acronym

A SUSAR meets three conditions:

- **Suspected**: a causal link with the medicine is suspected.
- **Unexpected**: the reaction is not expected given what is known about the product.
- **Serious**: it is a [serious adverse event](/en/glossaire/evenement-indesirable) (death, hospitalisation, life-threatening situation, etc.).

A serious but expected event, or one with no suspected link, is not a SUSAR.

## Reporting

SUSARs are subject to regulatory reporting within strict timelines (notably 7 or 15 days depending on severity), to the authorities and, in Europe, through EudraVigilance. It is a pillar of [pharmacovigilance](/en/glossaire/pharmacovigilance).

## The CRA's role

In the field, the CRA checks that adverse events are properly documented and escalated, and that potential SUSARs do not go unnoticed. It is one of the most sensitive points of [monitoring visits](/en/services/visites-de-monitoring).

For the full safety framework, see [clinical trial regulation](/en/blog/reglementation-des-essais-cliniques).

*Reference: ICH E6; clinical trial pharmacovigilance.*
