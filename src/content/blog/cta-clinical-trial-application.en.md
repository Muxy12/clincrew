The CTA (Clinical Trial Application) is the dossier requesting authorisation for a clinical trial. It is the essential regulatory step before any participant can be enrolled: no medicine trial starts without this authorisation.

## What a CTA contains

The application gathers everything needed to assess the trial:

- The [protocol](/en/glossaire/protocole) and its scientific justification
- The investigator's brochure (known data on the product)
- Information on the investigational product
- The documents for participants ([informed consent](/en/glossaire/consentement-eclaire))

## The CTIS portal

Since the [EU regulation (CTR)](/en/glossaire/reglement-europeen-essais-cliniques), the authorisation request for medicine trials is made through a single portal, CTIS, for all the member states concerned. The assessment is coordinated, with [ANSM](/en/glossaire/ansm) involved for France and the [EMA](/en/glossaire/ema) at European level.

## Beware the homonym

"CTA" can also mean Clinical Trial Agreement, the contract between the sponsor and the site (in France, often the [single agreement](/en/blog/convention-unique)). Application and Agreement are two different things: one authorises, the other contracts.

## CTA and start-up

Once the CTA is approved and the [ethics committee](/en/glossaire/cpp) opinion obtained, the study can start: site [selection](/en/services/visite-de-selection) and [initiation](/en/services/visite-d-initiation), then first enrolments.

*Reference: Regulation (EU) No 536/2014; CTIS portal.*
