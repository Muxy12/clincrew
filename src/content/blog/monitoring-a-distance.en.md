Remote monitoring complements on-site visits by shifting part of the oversight to data review and remote exchanges. Used well, it speeds up follow-up and cuts travel, without replacing everything that must be checked on site.

## What remote monitoring covers

- Centralised review of the study's data and indicators.
- Query handling and follow-up to resolution.
- Essential-document checks and [TMF / eTMF](/en/services/controle-documentation) follow-up.
- Regular exchanges with investigator teams between visits.

## Its limits

Some checks stay on site: source data verification where remote access is not permitted, and investigational product control (accountability, storage conditions). [Site management](/en/services/suivi-des-centres) therefore combines both approaches.

## A hybrid approach

In practice, the most effective model is hybrid: remote for data and documentation, on site for what requires a physical presence. [Risk-based monitoring](/en/blog/monitoring-base-sur-les-risques) helps decide what belongs to each mode.

## In short

- Remote monitoring covers data, queries and documentation.
- Source data and investigational product often stay on site.
- A risk-driven hybrid offers the best balance.
