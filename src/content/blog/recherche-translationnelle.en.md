Translational research bridges basic research (in the laboratory) and clinical research (in humans). Its ambition is often summed up as "from bench to bedside": turning a biological discovery into a concrete benefit for patients.

## What it covers

Translational research works in both directions:

- **From basic to clinical**: turning a biological target into a drug candidate, then into a [clinical trial](/en/glossaire/essai-clinique).
- **From clinical to basic**: feeding basic research with observations made in patients.

## Its place in a medicine's development

It sits upstream of the [clinical phases](/en/glossaire/phases-essai-clinique): biomarker identification, preclinical studies, first administrations in humans. This is often where the delicate move from proof of concept to a [phase I](/en/glossaire/phases-essai-clinique) trial is played out.

## Why it matters

Many drug candidates fail between the laboratory and the clinic. Translational research aims to better select what deserves to be tested in humans, and so to reduce costly failures.

## Translational research and monitoring

The first in-human studies arising from translational research are still full clinical trials: they demand the same monitoring rigour and the same compliance with [Good Clinical Practice](/en/glossaire/bonnes-pratiques-cliniques).

*Reference: biomedical research, medicine development.*
