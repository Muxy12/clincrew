A clinical trial can take place at a single site (single-centre study) or at several sites at once (multicentre study). This choice, defined in the [protocol](/en/glossaire/protocole), has concrete consequences for recruitment, logistics and monitoring.

## The single-centre study

It runs at one investigator site. Advantages: simple logistics, a single team, great consistency of practice. Limits: slower recruitment and sometimes less generalisable results, as they are tied to a single setting.

## The multicentre study

It involves several sites, often in several cities or countries. Advantages: faster recruitment and more representative results. Limits: heavier coordination, variability between sites to control, and [monitoring](/en/glossaire/monitoring-clinique) to deploy at every site.

## The impact on monitoring

The more sites there are, the more complex the follow-up: each site has its own [visits](/en/blog/les-4-visites-de-monitoring), [investigator](/en/glossaire/investigateur) and data to verify. This is precisely where [CRA](/en/glossaire/arc-promoteur) backup makes sense, to cover a batch of sites without burdening the structure.

## In short

Single-centre means simplicity, multicentre means power and representativeness. The choice depends on the study's objectives and the target population.
