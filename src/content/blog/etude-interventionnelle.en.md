An interventional study is research in which a treatment is given, a procedure is applied or a participant's care is modified as part of the study. In other words, the study intervenes on the participant: that is what distinguishes it from mere observation.

## What characterises an interventional study

- The allocation of a treatment or procedure decided by the protocol, not by routine care alone
- Added constraints or risks for the participant
- A reinforced regulatory framework

The medicine clinical trial is the typical example: a treatment is compared with a comparator, often through [randomisation](/en/glossaire/randomisation) and [blinding](/en/glossaire/insu-aveugle).

## Interventional or non-interventional?

Conversely, in [non-interventional research](/en/blog/recherche-non-interventionnelle), care is observed without being modified. In France, the [Jardé law](/en/blog/loi-jarde) distinguishes three categories by risk: interventional, with minimal risks and constraints, and non-interventional.

## What it implies

An interventional study requires [informed consent](/en/glossaire/consentement-eclaire), the opinion of an [ethics committee](/en/glossaire/cpp), the [ANSM](/en/glossaire/ansm) authorisation and rigorous follow-up. It is precisely this follow-up that the CRA performs during [monitoring visits](/en/services/visites-de-monitoring).
