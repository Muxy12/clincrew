A placebo is a treatment with no active ingredient, presented as the medicine under study. It serves as a comparator to distinguish the real effect of the medicine from the effect of simply being treated (the placebo effect). Its use is framed by ethics: it is used only when no essential reference treatment exists.

## What a placebo is for

- Measuring the medicine's own effect
- Acting as a comparator in a controlled trial
- Enabling [blinding](/en/glossaire/insu-aveugle)

## Placebo and comparator

When a reference treatment exists, it is often used as the comparator rather than a placebo. The choice is defined in the [protocol](/en/glossaire/protocole) and goes together with [randomisation](/en/glossaire/randomisation).

*Reference: clinical trial methodology.*
