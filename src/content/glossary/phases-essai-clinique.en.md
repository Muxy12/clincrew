The phases of a clinical trial describe the successive stages of a medicine's development, from its first administration in humans to its post-marketing follow-up. Each phase answers different questions and involves a growing number of participants.

## The four phases

- **Phase I**: tolerance and [pharmacokinetics](/en/glossaire/pharmacocinetique) in a small number of people.
- **Phase II**: dose finding and first signals of efficacy.
- **Phase III**: pivotal trials, often multicentre and [randomised](/en/glossaire/randomisation), supporting the [marketing authorisation](/en/glossaire/amm).
- **Phase IV**: real-world follow-up after marketing.

## The role of monitoring

Whatever the phase, site follow-up adapts to the study's risk level, through [monitoring visits](/en/services/visites-de-monitoring).

*Reference: ICH E6 (Good Clinical Practice).*
