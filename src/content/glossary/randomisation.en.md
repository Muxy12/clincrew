Randomisation, or random allocation, means assigning each participant's treatment at random. It spreads participants' characteristics comparably across the groups and removes selection bias. It is one of the pillars of a comparative trial's reliability.

## Why randomise

- Make the groups comparable at the start
- Eliminate bias in treatment allocation
- Strengthen the validity of the comparison

## Randomisation, blinding and placebo

Randomisation often combines with [blinding](/en/glossaire/insu-aveugle) and a comparator, which may be a [placebo](/en/glossaire/placebo). Together, these methods limit bias and strengthen the robustness of the results.

*Reference: clinical trial methodology.*
