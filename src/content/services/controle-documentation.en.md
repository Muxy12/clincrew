Clinical documentation control keeps your study ready to be inspected at any time. In clinical research, one rule holds: what is not documented did not happen. The investigator site file (ISF) at the site and the Trial Master File (TMF) at the sponsor are the study's memory — they must stay complete, current and traceable, in line with Good Clinical Practice (ICH E6).

## What the control covers

- Completeness and version of essential documents (protocol, consents, CVs, delegations)
- Consistency between the site's ISF and the sponsor's TMF
- Traceability: dating, signatures, version and amendment tracking
- Regulatory documents and their updates across the study
- Practical readiness for an audit or inspection

## Why it is a safety net

An incomplete TMF is the first inspection finding. Gaps are rarely spotted in one pass: they build up document after document. Regular control catches deviations early, while they are still easy to fix, rather than the night before an inspection.
